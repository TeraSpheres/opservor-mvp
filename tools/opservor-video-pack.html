Read this before you generate anything

Dana has never been generated. In the shot you already have she is only a blurred shoulder, so there is no face to stay consistent with. Her portrait is the first thing to make, and every later Dana shot must be asked for in that same chat. Get her right before anything else — she is in six of the thirteen shots.
Do not ask for a clock face showing 8:00. Image generators cannot place clock hands reliably — you will get 10:10 every time, because that is how clocks are photographed in advertising. The shot below asks for a digital desk clock, and even then you should plan to put 08:00 on screen as a caption in CapCut. Guaranteed correct, and it reads as deliberate rather than as set dressing.
Nobody's mouth has to match the words. Every talking shot below is framed wide, turned away, or over a shoulder, and the dialogue rides as voice-over with captions. This is how most corporate film is cut, it looks more expensive, and it removes the one step that never comes out right.

The 500-character limit that stopped you before is per text block, not per project. The longest line here is 62 characters. Generate each caption's speech from its own text block and you will never come near it.
Punctuation is your timing control. Text-to-speech paces off full stops. "We had the part on the shelf. The system said so all week." breathes at that full stop; take it out and the line runs together. If a voice rushes, add punctuation rather than re-recording.

Put the two spoken lines in quote marks on screen and the narration without. It is a small thing and it does a lot — the viewer stops wondering who is talking.

In CapCut

  1. New project, ratio 1:1. Square. Your images are landscape, so square crops off far less than vertical, and LinkedIn treats them the same.
  2. Import all thirteen clips and lay them out in the order above. Seven are the ones you are about to generate; six you already have. The evening shot is used twice — once as the closing image and again, darkened, under the end card.
  3. Set the durations from the timecodes on each card. They are not all equal — the finding gets eight seconds and "Go on." gets four.
  4. Add the movement. Select a clip → Animation → Zoom in or Pan. Slow. If you notice the move, it is too fast.
  5. Add the captions — one text block per shot, lower third, large, held for the whole clip. No word-by-word animation. Quote marks on the spoken lines.
  6. Add the 08:00 and the logo over shot three, top left. Time in a plain monospace font, LOGO-opservor.png directly beneath it at about 8% of the frame width, sharing the same left edge. Fade them in together.
  7. Generate the speech from each text block: Text to speech in the text panel. Narrator voice for the observations, one voice each for Dana and Marcus.
  8. Add music, no lyrics, at about 20%. Search "corporate documentary" or "cinematic tension". It sits under the voices, never against them.
  9. Export 1080×1080, 30fps and upload to LinkedIn as a video post.
